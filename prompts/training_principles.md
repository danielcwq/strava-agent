# Training Principles — Stable Layer

Durable principles the agent reasons *against*. This is **not** a workout calendar
and **not** a list of exact prescriptions — it is the stable layer. Dynamic state
(recent sessions, today's readiness, current load) comes from the data context,
not from here.

For *facts*, the data wins. For *how to decide*, these principles win.

## Current phase

- **Phase:** half-marathon-specific build.
- **Primary goal:** increase total *controlled* work near HM / threshold effort.
- **Secondary goal:** build weekly volume and long-run durability.
- **Race:** SF 2nd Half / City Half — see project context for race detail.

## Weekly rhythm — day *roles*, not a fixed plan

The runner trains by feel, but the week has a usual shape. These are **default
roles**, not commitments — readiness, weather, and life legitimately shift them.
The data context reports today's role directly (`training_snapshot.today_role`);
trust that over inferring from the date.

- **Mon** — easy or off. Its job is to *protect Tuesday*.
- **Tue** — the key quality day, usually CCSF track workouts targetting HM / 10k specific speed.
- **Wed–Fri** — easy or off.
- **Sat** — long run, often with steady or progression elements.
- **Sun** — off.

**The load-bearing rule:** a support day stays a support day even when readiness
is green. Green numbers on a Monday mean "Tuesday's session will go well" — not
"do a quality session today." Readiness decides *how hard a day's role is run*,
not *whether to overwrite the role*.

Override a day's role only when the **rhythm itself was disrupted** — the key
session was missed, or travel / illness shifted the week. A good HRV reading is
not a disruption.

## Progression — change one variable at a time

When stepping up a key session, move **one** variable, in this order:

1. **Total controlled work volume** — more total km at effort. *Move this first.*
2. **Rep length** — lengthen individual reps.
3. **Pace** — increase pace. *Move this last.*

In an HM-specific build, durability at effort matters more than peak rep speed.
Most progressions should be "more controlled work," not "faster." Again, the goal here is to build tolerance at HM race pace.

## What to avoid

- Chasing faster reps when total volume can still progress.
- Turning an HM-specific session into a VO2 workout (5K / mile-pace reps).
- Stacking cardiac stress — a hard quality day directly after a high-HR long run.
- Prescribing exact paces or rep counts off a single green HRV reading.

## Workout grammar — HM-specific quality work

A grammar for *constructing* a sensible next session, not a menu to pick from.

- **Effort band:** HM pace up to slightly faster than HM (≈10K effort at the
  sharp end). **Not** 5K / VO2.
- **Useful shapes:** long reps ~2–4 km · cruise reps ~1–2 km · continuous tempo blocks.
- **Rest:** short jog / walk, just enough to hold rhythm — roughly 4:1 to 6:1 work:rest.
- **Progression unit:** total controlled work volume.

Worked example of the reasoning (illustrative, not the current state): if the last
HM-specific session was 3×2k — 6 km of work — the next useful step is ~7–9 km of
controlled work at the *same* effort: 4×2k, 3×3k, or 2×3k + 2×1k. The step is
*more work*, not *faster 2k's*. When several shapes satisfy the same purpose,
offer 2–3 and say what each trades off (3×3k = cleanest sustained rhythm; 4×2k =
lower risk, easier to control).
